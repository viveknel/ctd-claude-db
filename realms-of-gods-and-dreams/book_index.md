# In the Realm of Gods and Dreams — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
*In the Realm of Gods and Dreams* (v2.1, 2018/2024) is a licensed
fan-made (Storyteller's Vault) supplement by Sebastian Noh that
reimagines Changeling: The Dreaming for East, South, and Southeast
Asian myth and folklore. Its explicit framing (Introduction, "An
Important Notice," p.9) is that it is "not your average Changeling
supplement" but "a total setting and system rebuild" — it renames and
reworks the core concepts (fae become the **Little Gods**; Banality
becomes **Defilement**; the mortal world seen truly is **the golden
world**; kith becomes **Kinship**; cantrips split into **Weaving** and
**Unleashing**) while remaining explicitly designed for direct use
alongside a core Changeling rulebook — ideally Changeling: The Dreaming
20th Anniversary Edition, per its own recommendation. The book covers
setting/cosmology (Ch.1), the social structure of Little God society —
Incarnations, Kinships, Karmas (Ch.2), character creation (Ch.3), the
elemental Arts magic system (Ch.4), Storytelling advice including a
direct C20 conversion guide (Ch.5), a large regional catalog of example
Kinships and story hooks organized by country (Ch.6), antagonists and a
Storyteller-character toolkit (Ch.7), and two appendices: statted
conversions of core-book Gallain/Kithain fae into this book's rules
(App. I) and errata/bonus material including a new playable Half-Devil
template (App. II).

## Front Matter (pp. 1-6)
Title page, a short evocative poem ("The gates of heaven are closed..."),
credits (written by Sebastian Noh/Hsienfan; art from multiple named
artists plus classic art selected from earlier Changeling/Kindred of the
East-line books), and version history (original release → Version 2:
revised Defilement-accrual rules, ad hoc Kinship rules, Appendix II added
→ Version 2.1: further corrections). Copyright notice confirms this is
licensed Storyteller's Vault content using White Wolf/World of Darkness
IP, not an Onyx Path-published core book. **Authorship note:** the
credits ("Written by: Sebastian Noh (Hsienfan)... Developed by: Sebastian
Noh, with assistance from RPGnet") match the credits of the fan-made
Vampire: The Masquerade supplement *Kindred of the East: The Relentless
Age* ("Written By: hsienfan / Developed By: hsienfan, with assistance
from RPGnet"), which shares the same pseudonym, the same "developed with
RPGnet" collaboration model, and the same named collaborators (Coco
Vanille, Tommy Lee) — strong evidence the two books share an author. See
this bundle's README for the full comparison.

## Introduction: Land of Endless Dreams (pp. 7-18)
**Summary:** Opens with a fiction vignette (a drowning smuggler saved
and transformed by mermaid-like "merfolk" gods on the Mekong River),
then lays out the book's premise, chapter roadmap, an inspirational-media
list (folklore sources like *The Journey to the West*; anime/film
touchstones including *Spirited Away*, *Big Trouble in Little China*,
*Frieren*, *Mushishi*, *Death Parade*), and — most load-bearing for
future lookups — a full **Lexicon** (p.11-16) defining every major
in-book term in one place: Adhene, Age of Beautiful Sadness/Falling
Darkness/Legends/One-Becoming-Many/Sorrows (this book's five-age
cosmological timeline), Anchor, Beloved/Quickened/Revived/Enshrined/
Rampant (the five Incarnations), the Changeling Way, Daitya/Mara
(Unhallowed paths), Defilement, the Dream-Current, Echo, Enchantment,
Fortune, Frailty, Freehold, Glamour, God-Blooded/God-Touched, the Golden
World, Grotto-heaven, Guru Realm, Half-Devil, Harvesting, Incarnation,
Karma, Kinship, the Lower/Upper Heavens, Mists, Prayer, Revelry, the
Sacred Incarnations, Shattering, Sundering, Thallain, the Thousand
Hells, Trod, Unhallowed, UnIncarnated, Unleashing, Weaving, Wisdom/
Wonder/Wrath (the three Forms), Wizard, Yama King, and Zapathasura
(a named demon prince — see Chapter One). Also includes an explicit
**On Cultural License** section (p.17) with real-world guidance on
running games sensitively across Asian cultures, summarizing points from
*Kindred of the East: The Relentless Age*'s Storytelling chapter (treat
people as more similar than different across settings; tread carefully
around real oppressed-minority struggles; prioritize being able to
course-correct over getting everything perfect).
**Key themes:** explicit "total rebuild, not reskin" framing; real-world
cultural sensitivity as an authorial concern, not an afterthought.
**Key terms/entities:** full Lexicon (p.11-16), Cultural License
guidance (p.17).

## Chapter One: The Golden World (pp. 19-40)
**Summary:** The setting/cosmology chapter. Opens with an in-fiction
myth of the gods' primordial wars, humanity's rise, an attempted
mortal-immortal rebellion against heaven, and the **Shattering** — "under
a darkened sun... during the rampage of the Great Khan" (a 13th-century
dating consistent with the core C20 book's own Shattering timeline) —
when magic retreated and the gates of heaven slammed shut. Defines **the
golden world** as true reality as the Little Gods see it, obscured for
mortals by **the Mists** (Defilement's perception-veil, functionally
equivalent to C20's Mists/Banality-blindness). Explains **Incarnation**
in detail: unIncarnated Little Gods linger in the Lower Heavens until
called by a "thread of destiny" toward an **Anchor** (host); Incarnation
requires the ongoing fulfillment of **prayers** in exchange for
**Glamour**. Distinguishes the brief, mortal **Human Incarnations**
(Beloved/Quickened/Revived — see Ch.2 for full mechanics) from the
longer-lived **Sacred Incarnations** (Enshrined/Rampant). Introduces the
**Karmas** (five philosophical schools — detailed in Ch.2) and **Circles**
(the basic social unit, an oath-bound group formed around a shared
prayer or purpose — this book's rough equivalent of a motley). A full
sidebar, **The Nature of Defilement** (p.24), defines Defilement with
concrete worked examples (a traumatized boarding school, a
blood-drinking "Hungry Dead" monster, a cursed blade forged from
hatred) and states its deepest danger is preventing humanity from
perceiving its own world truly. Covers relations with other World of
Darkness supernaturals in detail: **ghosts** (a two-souled, "passionate
vs. hateful" model of the Underworld's Tempest and Dark Kingdoms,
distinct from Wraith: The Oblivion's Shroud/Skinlands-Shadowlands split
— a sidebar, "What's a Gauntlel?", explicitly notes the Little Gods'
different, roads-and-doorways cosmology of realm-crossing); the
**Hungry Dead** (this book's vampires — condemned revenants who can
paradoxically see the golden world naturally, without Enchantment,
making for careful mutual diplomacy with the Little Gods); wizards and
the hypocritical, Defilement-saturated **Technocratic Society**; the
**Half-Devils** (demon-human hybrids, formerly pitiful mutated outcasts,
now — since the turning of the age — dangerous and urbane, detailed
further in App. II); and the **Beast Courts** (a shapeshifter
confederation defending the **Spirit Wilds**, whose corrupted deep
region is called **Malfeas**). A **Strange Cousins** section (p.35-36)
briefly introduces the C20-derived fae tribes (Adhene, Inanimae, Kithain,
Merfolk, Nunnehi — fully converted to this book's rules in Appendix I).
Closes with **The Journey to Heaven** (p.37-38): Freeholds as
Trod-crossing stations; the Lower Heavens (also called **the Dreaming**
by Dreamborn Little Gods and by "the Kithain further west" — an explicit
naming crosswalk to the core book); and the Upper Heavens, whose gates
opened twice since the Shattering — the **Resurgence** (1969, the Moon
Landing) and, thirty years later in 1999, the rise and defeat of the
demon prince **Zapathasura**, marking the start of the **Age of
Sorrows**. Note: this 1999 dating for the "turning of the age" is
notably different from the C20 core rulebook's own dating of its
parallel event (the Evanescence) to September 11, 2001 — see this
library's top-level `library_index.md` for discussion.
**Key themes:** the golden world as a hidden layer of literal reality
rather than a separate plane; Incarnation as a transactional
bargain (prayers for Glamour) that structures every other system in the
book; real-world historical moments (the Moon Landing, a fictional 1999
demon-war) as literal setting-shaping magical events, paralleling the
core C20 book's own use of history.
**Key terms/entities:** the Shattering (p.19), Incarnation and Anchors
(p.22-23), the Karmas and Circles (p.23, 25-26), Defilement (p.21,
24), ghosts/Underworld cosmology (p.29-31), the Hungry Dead (p.31-33),
Half-Devils (p.33-34), the Beast Courts and Spirit Wilds (p.34-35),
Strange Cousins (p.35-36), the Resurgence and the Age of Sorrows
(p.37-39), Zapathasura (p.38-39).

## Chapter Two: Families of the Gods (pp. 41-86)
**Summary:** The character-concept chapter — Incarnations, Kinships,
and Karmas in full mechanical and narrative detail. This is the
single most load-bearing chapter for character-building questions.

**The Five Incarnations** (p.44-55), each given full narrative
treatment plus a Luck (starting mechanical benefit) and Curse
(what happens at high Defilement): **Beloved** (a god and a human share
one body/mind as co-equals; Curse splits them into either a
sociopathic god-in-control or a magic-blind, dissociated human-in-control
state on alternating turns); **Quickened** (the god is born and raised
fully human, only awakening around adolescence in a "Chrysalis"-like
event; Curse causes total forgetting — a mundane life with no memory of
godhood); **Revived** (the god takes a dying mortal's body and inherits
a flood of the deceased's memories and unfinished business; Curse causes
the body to visibly decay and stop healing); **Enshrined** (the god
bonds to an object or place, is normally incorporeal, and must spend
Glamour to craft a temporary human vessel; Curse is an unwakable,
magic-immune slumber); **Rampant** (the god bonds to an animal, gaining
unusually long animal lifespans; Curse traps them as a feral, near-
mindless beast). A separate **Unhallowed** section (p.54) names the
**Three Unhallowed Errors** — Indulgence (becoming a Daitya, mastering
rather than surrendering to Defilement), Obeisance (swearing fealty to
a Yama King), and Cruelty (consuming a human soul outright, linked to
rumors about the invading **Lunarian Host**) — with full antagonist
rules deferred to Chapter Seven.

**Kinships** (p.42, 56-74): the Little Gods' answer to kith, divided
into three **tribes** — **Celestial** (heaven-exiled, honor-bound),
**Earthblooded** (self-styled original rulers of earth), and
**Dreamborn** (believe themselves born from the human unconscious/
Dream-Current) — each represented by three fully statted example
Kinships: *Celestial* — **Apsara** (beautiful, radiant sky-maiden
diplomats, currently splitting internally over the invading, darker
Lunarian Apsara), **Qilin** (luck-bestowing "Chinese unicorn"
kingmakers, pacifist, capstone ability to assist any other character's
roll), **Kinnara** (bird-winged performer-gods who "gave humans the
gift of music," vulnerable when physically confined); *Earthblooded* —
**Yaksha** (monstrous, horned, perpetually-hungry brute-force gods,
Japan's Oni among them, prone to battle-frenzy), **Kaibyou** (lordly
cat-spirits who secretly rule housecats and strays), **Jinn** (smokeless-
fire shapeshifters/knowledge-hoarders, prone to reckless fixation);
*Dreamborn* — **Immortal Sages** (self-made, treasure-anchored
immortals wrestling with purpose now that their corrupted cousins the
Hungry Dead have overrun the world), **Shinigami** (Japanese death-gods,
unsettling by nature, can compel ghosts and show mortals visions of
their own death), **Hobs** (small, boggan-cousin domestic-service fae
who stayed in Asia rather than joining the European Changeling Way,
compulsively helpful).

**The Five Karmas** (p.43, 75-85) — this book's Court/philosophy
system, explicitly non-binary (five schools, not a Seelie/Unseelie
split), each with Karmic Principles and named example Circles:
**Blood Garlands** ("Black Sutra"/School of Audacity — power without
apology, treat pleasure and pain as equally valid, enforce one's word
as law); **Dancing Bells** ("School of Impermanence" — honor the
present Incarnation and mortal connections above abstract heavenly
duty); **Lions of Heaven** ("Pure Jade Vehicle"/School of Destiny —
proud agents of heaven's literal bureaucracy, purity-focused); **Uncrowned
Thunders** ("Diamond-Bolt Throne"/School of Insight — radical
transformation and the destruction of ignorance/subjugation as sacred
duty); **Wandering Pearls** ("Eternal Dream-Wheel"/School of
Remembrance — bearing witness to the world, honoring memory, accepting
endings). A Little God may follow one Karma, switch, or follow none;
practicing one's Karmic Principles earns a spendable "token of merit"
usable to dissolve Defilement, block a Curse, or negate a botched
Unleashing.
**Key themes:** every Incarnation's mechanical Curse is a specific,
thematically-fitted failure mode (forgetting, decay, slumber, feral
loss of self) rather than a generic penalty; the three-tribe/five-Karma
matrix gives Little God society two independent, cross-cutting axes of
identity (what kind of exile you believe yourself to be, and what
philosophy you've chosen), richer than C20's single Seelie/Unseelie
axis.
**Key terms/entities:** the five Incarnations and their Lucks/Curses
(p.44-55), the Three Unhallowed Errors (p.54), the three Kinship tribes
and nine example Kinships (p.56-74), the five Karmas and example
Circles (p.75-85).

## Chapter Three: Character Creation & Rules (pp. 87-132)
**Summary:** Mostly mechanical (step-by-step character creation using
the same Attributes/Abilities structure as Changeling: The Dreaming 20th
Anniversary Edition, explicitly cross-referenced as the compatible core
book) — not summarized trait-by-trait here; query book_chunks.db for
specific rules text. Narratively relevant content embedded in this
chapter: the full 26-Legacy list carried over from Changeling (Seelie
and Unseelie Legacies reused as Willpower-regain archetypes, e.g. Beast,
Bumpkin, Paladin, Panderer, Squire); special Backgrounds unique to this
book — **Freehold** (with a "Balefire" and required "Act of Devotion"
per Freehold, several named example Freeholds given, e.g. Sanzugawa Boat
Town, Little Patala), **Remembrance**, **Treasure** (example: Marid's
Spearhead), **Legendry**; the **Forms** (Wisdom/Wonder/Wrath) as
prayer/magic-affinity categories tied to character concept; the full
**Glamour economy** (temporary vs. permanent track, "cashing in" ten
temporary points into a permanent dot via prayer-fulfillment) and
**Defilement economy** (mirrored temporary/permanent track, with
Curses and optional **Echoes** — a way to crystallize Defilement into a
fixed taboo/deformity instead of risking a full Curse); a step-by-step
**Prayer** system (p.116-122, 5 levels of prayer scale from personal
wishes to household-spanning Duties, with a full worked example);
sample **Merits** (e.g. "Glamourous Incarnation," raising starting
Glamour to 5); custom-Kinship creation guidance with a worked example
(a player-created "Kappa" Kinship); and a "High Glamour" discussion of
what powerful late-game Little Gods look like.
**Key themes:** N/A (mechanical chapter) apart from the Glamour/
Defilement dual-track economy and prayer system, both of which are this
book's distinctive spins on C20's Glamour/Banality and Backgrounds.
**Key terms/entities:** the 26 Legacies (reused from Changeling core,
p.91-92), Freehold/Treasure/Remembrance/Legendry Backgrounds (p.98-104),
Forms (p.92-93 cross-ref), Glamour and Defilement tracks (p.108-111),
Echoes (p.128-129), the Prayer system (p.116-122).

## Chapter Four: Arts (pp. 133-188)
**Summary:** The magic-system chapter — mechanically dense (8 elemental
Arts, each with a full Weaving catalog), comparable to the core
rulebook's Chapter Four, and not summarized power-by-power here; query
book_chunks.db for specific Weaving/Unleashing text. System overview
worth knowing: every Art pairs a **worldly element** (a physical
domain) with a **subtle element** (an emotional/abstract domain) — the
**8 Arts** are **Beasts** (animal life; vitality/instinct), **Blossoms**
(plants/wood; creativity), **Embers** (fire; fury), **Metal** (metal;
authority), **Shadows** (void/darkness/silence; fear), **Stones**
(earth; resilience), **Tempests** (air; clarity), and **Waves** (water;
connection). Magic is channeled through one of the three **Forms**
(Wisdom/Wonder/Wrath — see Ch.3), and effects scale on a 5-level
**Intensity** ladder (from simple "Awaken Element" communication/minor-
nudge effects at Intensity 1 up to "Enlighten Element" — creating a
sapient magical being — at Intensity 5) rather than by dot rating, which
instead governs control and risk. A **Weaving** is a pre-learned,
reliable, "practiced spell" version of an effect; an **Unleashing** is
improvised raw magic that risks extra Defilement or an uncontrolled
side effect if the roll's successes exceed the character's dots in the
Art. **Modifiers** (Circumstance, Continuance, Distance, Domain,
Judgment) add scope/duration/range/power at a Glamour surcharge, and
**Fortunes** (symbolic resonant items or actions, capped per scene at
the character's Glamour rating) discount the Glamour cost — with an
explicit note (p.139) that the underlying philosophy split over how
Fortunes work (the traditional "Harmonious Theory"/ritual-purity
approach vs. the post-Resurgence, Kithain-influenced "Theory of Bunks,"
where disruptive rather than solemn actions work just as well) directly
echoes the core C20 book's own Bunk mechanic. Several fully worked
dice-rolling examples are given throughout (a Jinn asking the wind for
news; an Apsara Unleashing fire against Skitterlings; a Garuda's fear
magic countered by a Shinigami). A **Grand Weaving** system (p.183-185)
lets multiple Artists pool Glamour and Fortunes for effects "too
expensive... for a single Artist to reasonably pull off," with a
detailed worked example (the Izumo Shrine's "Month of Gods" gathering).
Closes with **guidance for designing custom Arts** (p.186-188, worked
example: a player designs the Art of Roads), recommending new Arts be
conceived in thematically-linked families rather than ad hoc.
**Key themes:** N/A (mechanical chapter) — but note the explicit
Fortune/Bunk cross-reference to C20 as a rare moment where this book
names its mechanical debt to the core rulebook directly.
**Key terms/entities:** the 8 Arts and their element pairs (p.134,
152-181), Forms/Intensity/Weaving/Unleashing systems (p.134-141),
Modifiers and Fortunes (p.136-139), Grand Weavings (p.183-185), custom
Art design (p.186-188).

## Chapter Five: Storytelling in the Golden World (pp. 189-202)
**Summary:** Storytelling advice, comparable in role to the core
book's Chapter Eight, but with one section of major cross-reference
value: pages 197-201 give an explicit **conversion guide** for using
Little God characters and setting material directly with Changeling:
The Dreaming 20th Anniversary Edition's default rules (mapping
Intensity to C20's Art dot levels, mapping the eight elemental Arts
onto C20's Realm/Art structure, etc.) — the practical payoff of the
book's stated design goal of full cross-compatibility. Other content:
advice on running Bygones and encounters with them (evoking a
"mysterious and folkloric" mood, like meeting a fox in the woods, while
remembering most Bygones were never human and have their own rules to
respect); a worked **Touchstone** example (Willpower-regain tied to a
meaningful person, object, or place — here, a samurai's ancestral
summer home) paralleling C20's own touchstone-adjacent mechanics; rules
and mood guidance for the deeper Lower Heavens (Shallows vs. Depths,
dream-storm frequency, an example Grotto-Heaven called Moon Rabbit's
Mortar); and notes on running Kinships like the Shinigami as more niche
but more powerful given their unique perceptual Birthrights.
**Key themes:** explicit design-level cross-compatibility with C20
(the conversion guide is this book's clearest single piece of evidence
for how it relates to the core rulebook).
**Key terms/entities:** the C20 conversion guide (p.197-201), Touchstones
(p.192-193), Grotto-Heavens/Shallows/Depths (p.195-196).

## Chapter Six: All Things in Heaven & Earth (pp. 203-250)
**Summary:** A large, geography-organized catalog of regional example
Kinships, Circles, prayers, and story hooks spanning South, Southeast,
and East Asia — structurally analogous to the core C20 book's Concordia
gazetteer (Ch.1) but organized around named Kinships rather than
kingdoms. Dozens of fully statted regional Kinships are given by
country/culture, including (non-exhaustively): **India** — Manussiha
(guardian lion-spirits), Vanara (monkey-folk descended from the
Ramayana), Elder Yaksha (nature-flower spirits distinct from Chapter
Two's Yaksha), Nak (water-summoning nagas), Ghost-Tiger, and the
Bhakti-village God-Blooded; **Southeast Asia** — Orang Bunian
(near-human forest fae of Indonesia/Malaysia), Anghel and the
Merlion (a named, city-bound tutelary spirit of Singapore, forbidden
ever to leave the "Lion City"), mermaids; **China** — Huli Jing (fox
spirits), Jiaoren (silk-weaving merfolk), Jiangshi ("hopping corpse"
undead-adjacent gods), Yeren (wildman/yeti-type beings), Imugi
(dragon-in-waiting serpents), Gumiho (Korean nine-tailed-fox
cousins with a "visceral hunger" frailty); **Korea** — Dokkaebi
(goblin party-spirits), Josang (ancestor-spirits), Joseung Saja
(bureaucratic death-guides); **Japan** — Raijuu (thunder-beasts),
Tenko (prophetic foxes), Binbougami (poverty gods), Bake-danuki
(mischief-loving raccoon-dog tricksters), Yuki-onna (once-monstrous
snow women), the All-Japan Electric Power Society (a named Circle of
infrastructure-protecting Little Gods) and the twice-yearly Great Yokai
Market in Shinsaibashi (with named Treasures for sale, e.g. the
"Countess" fountain pen). Interwoven with these are larger historical/
mythic set-pieces functioning as ready-made campaign hooks: Yu Huang's
historical conquest and devastation of the Underworld's western isle of
Stygia; "the Dream of San Francisco," a Shining Host power struggle over
the Bay Area; Vietnam-era communist binding of Little Gods' souls to
trees as war-effort fuel; and closing sections recapping and expanding
the **Rhapsody** Glamour-gathering method (destroying an over-fed
mortal artist's masterpiece for a massive Glamour payout) with explicit
systems for its petitioner/relationship requirements.
**Key themes:** this chapter is the practical, ready-to-run payoff of
the book's cultural-specificity approach flagged in the Introduction —
treat it as reference/lookup material for a specific country or Kinship
rather than a connected narrative; query book_chunks.db by Kinship or
place name for full write-ups.
**Key terms/entities:** dozens of regional Kinships (p.204-234, see
summary above for named highlights), named example Circles and
Treasures (the All-Japan Electric Power Society p.239-240, the Great
Yokai Market p.241-243, the Countess fountain pen p.241-242), Rhapsody
systems recap (p.247-249).

## Chapter Seven: Antagonists & Storyteller Characters (pp. 251-302)
**Summary:** Threats and an NPC-building toolkit. **God-Touched and
God-Blooded** mortals (p.252-256) get full mechanical treatment as
special-mortal templates, up to a 7-point Merit tier that grants
Unleashing access and a Greater Birthright. **Harvesters** (p.256-260)
— hedge witches, black-market "god-eater" traffickers — get a
"Complete Consumption" system: eating specific parts of specific Little
God types grants the Harvester that Kinship's Art and Form (e.g.
consuming Mermaid Flesh grants the Art of Waves and Form of Wonder;
consuming a Shinigami Eye grants Shadows and Wisdom). Full **Defilement
mechanics for antagonists** (p.262-266) walk through escalating
severity, including the detail that a Defiled being at 7+ dots triggers
Defilement in observers simply by being present. The **Unhallowed
Incarnations** get full antagonist rules: **Daityas** (p.268-274, a
fully worked example, "The Regular," a Defilement-10 bartender-demon
with named former Echoes) and **Maras**/the Infernal Mysteries (p.274-
277, practitioners of forbidden hell-merging magic who can stave off
their Curse by embracing further corruption instead). A generic
**Storyteller-character build system** (p.277-296) for Bygones, Living
Prayers, and other magical creatures uses a simplified Rank (1-4+) and
Power-point framework instead of full character sheets, illustrated
with numerous named antagonists and allies: Queen Skitterling (a
Rank-2 Bygone), a fortune-hunting crow-Bygone, a Chinese-zodiac hybrid
creature, a Samé-Bito Guide archmaster, and a full Kithain/Sidhe
crossover NPC — a Seelie sidhe (Primary Legacy Peacock, Secondary
Legacy Courtier) who Incarnated in Asia rather than Concordia,
explicitly demonstrating cross-setting compatibility with C20's own
Noble Houses. Closes with a full **Half-Devil antagonist writeup**
(demon-human hybrids with an "Apocalyptic Form" transformation, later
expanded into a full player-character option in Appendix II).
**Key themes:** antagonist design throughout is built around
transformation/corruption of otherwise-sympathetic beings (Harvesters
were once desperate demon-hunters; Daityas and Maras are Little Gods who
made a specific wrong choice; the Defiled scale explicitly separates
"apathetic" low-grade corruption from "intentional, cultivated" high-
grade corruption) rather than purely external monstrosity.
**Key terms/entities:** God-Touched/God-Blooded Merits (p.252-256),
Harvesters and Complete Consumption (p.256-260), Daityas and Maras
(p.268-277), the Rank/Power Storyteller-character system (p.277-296),
the crossover Sidhe NPC example (p.297-300), Half-Devils (p.300-302,
expanded p.324-328).

## Appendix I: Strange Cousins (pp. 303-316)
**Summary:** Full statted conversions of fae originally detailed in
Changeling: The Dreaming, adapted to this book's rules — the clearest
single piece of evidence in the book for its intended use as a
direct C20 expansion rather than a standalone product. Converted:
**Elegbara** (Eshu-cousin African "wanderer" Kithain, road- and
story-based magic, among the first to join the Changeling Way);
**Fir-bholg** (Adhene/Dark-kin wild gods exiled after losing an ancient
war, sacrifice-hungry, command Trods with uncanny accuracy); **Kokua**
(Menehune warrior-activists, athletically gifted, prone to cruelty in
victory); **Mannikin** (Inanimae of the "Empire of Dolls," bound to
human-shaped objects, emotionally dependent on the humans who "chose"
them); **Nanehi** (Nunnehi wanderers devoted to protecting their
tribe, vain about their appearance); **Redcap** (evolved from
solitary castle-haunting predators into primal fear-embodying hunters
via the Dream-Current, ravenous for raw flesh); **Satyr** (revelry and
intoxication spirits, prone to emotional extremes tied to their
Legacies); **Syrinx** (Merfolk nobility of House Melusine, currently
battling an invading "Mother of the Depths" and her Merrow armies,
allying with regional Little Gods out of necessity); **Goblin**
(malicious, accident-prone tinkerer-fae split between the Thallain's
Red and Green Courts); and — with the most extensive unique mechanical
treatment — **the Thallain** themselves (p.312-314), given a wholly
new **Court Mantle** Background/conversion system: three courts (Red/
war, White/coldness-cruelty, Green/mystification) tied to three
"Emanations" (Scarlet, Alabaster, Virescent) of what this book calls
the "Goddess of Defilement" — notably a different cosmological framing
of Thallain origin than the core C20 rulebook's own Fomorian/Elder-Dark
account, though both books agree the Thallain descend from a corrupted
reading of a shared cursed text tradition. A Thallain with a Court
Mantle permanently sacrifices both ordinary Legacies in exchange for
Legendry-equivalent protection and Court-aligned Unleashing bonuses.
**Key themes:** this appendix is the book's most explicit statement of
its relationship to the core rulebook — treat it as a translation layer,
and note the Thallain entry specifically as a point of *setting*
divergence worth flagging (see this library's `library_index.md`).
**Key terms/entities:** all ten converted kiths (p.304-314, see summary
above), the Court Mantle system and its three Courts/Emanations
(p.312-313).

## Appendix II: Errata and Additions (pp. 317-330)
**Summary:** Post-release clarifications plus substantial bonus
material. **Clarifications**: an expanded, previously-mythic third
Unhallowed technique, **body-snatching via Rhapsody** (a Little God can
use a destroyed mortal-artist masterpiece as a ritual weapon to steal
the artist's soul and body outright — treated in-fiction as a capital
crime, rumored to be secretly practiced by the invading Lunarian Host);
**Bygone life-and-death rules** (magical creatures gaining Defilement
beyond their Will/Glamour rating risk death or corruption into a
nightmare monster); **Glamour Zero: The Prayerless** (a new mortal
state — total, silent apathy with no capacity to lower one's own
Defilement — that draws the Little Gods' attention and can only be
broken by Enchantment and an epic quest); explicit **guidance comparing
the Defilement scale to C20's Banality scale** (p.321, noting Defilement
is expected to run much lower by default — most mortals sit at 3-4 dots
rather than a mid-range Banality score); a mass-effect **Defilement-
accrual chart** for magic performed in front of large unEnchanted
crowds; rules for **achieving magical permanency** (extended-duration
Continuance costs, and how a magical creation can "root itself" into a
true Living Prayer or Habitat-bound creature); and **Master Daitya
territory-expansion rules**, plus **special per-tribe benefits** for
each Strange Cousin fae type (Adhene possession mechanics, Inanimae
century-scale Defilement-shedding slumber, Kithain's easier
cross-Kinship trust-building, Merfolk deep-water Freeholds, Nunnehi
tribal-land Glamour). **Bonus Material**: a full **Half-Devil player-
character template** (p.324-328) — Willpower/Majesty(Glamour-analog)/
Torment(Defilement-analog) tempers, a customizable "Apocalyptic Form"
built from a Major Feature plus two Minor Features (example named
forms: Beetle-Body Enforcer, Gelatinous Horror, Parasite God,
Pitch-Black Archer, Siren of the Deep, Visage of Agony), and a "Pacting
with Mortals" system for trading life-changing miracles for worship-
derived Majesty — plus **two new example Arts** designed to
demonstrate the custom-Art guidance from Chapter Four: the **Art of
Dreams** (worldly: dream-environments; subtle: credulity) and the
**Art of Fates** (worldly: luck/chance; subtle: subjective time
perception).
**Key themes:** the explicit C20 Banality-scale comparison (p.321) is
this book's single most direct piece of guidance for translating
between the two systems' severity curves.
**Key terms/entities:** Rhapsody body-snatching (p.318-320), Glamour
Zero/the Prayerless (p.320-321), the Defilement-vs-Banality comparison
note (p.321), per-tribe Strange Cousin benefits (p.323-324), Half-Devil
player template and Apocalyptic Form (p.324-328), the Art of Dreams and
Art of Fates (p.328-330).

## Cross-cutting themes (whole book)
- **A total rebuild that still wants to interoperate**: the book
  repeatedly does two things at once — replacing C20 terminology
  wholesale (Banality→Defilement, kith→Kinship, Court→Karma,
  cantrip→Weaving/Unleashing) while also building explicit bridges back
  to the core rulebook (the Ch.5 conversion guide, the Ch.7 crossover
  Sidhe NPC, the full Appendix I kith conversions, the Appendix II
  Banality-scale comparison note). Treat it less as an alternate universe
  and more as a fully-dressed regional expansion that happens to use its
  own vocabulary.
- **Transactional exchange as the core metaphysical engine**:
  Incarnation-for-prayers-for-Glamour (Ch.1-3) structures nearly every
  other system in the book, down to Harvesters trading body parts for
  Arts (Ch.7) and Half-Devils trading miracles for worship (App. II) —
  everything of value in this setting is earned through an exchange, not
  simply possessed.
- **Corruption as escalating intentionality, not a binary state**: the
  Defilement scale (Ch.3, Ch.7, App. II) explicitly distinguishes
  passive/apathetic low-grade corruption from deliberate, cultivated
  high-grade corruption (the "nurse" and "loan shark" examples,
  App. II) — mirroring the core C20 book's own insistence that Banality
  isn't simply about being unimaginative.
- **Geographic and cultural specificity as a design principle**: Chapter
  Six's country-by-country Kinship catalog and the explicit Cultural
  License guidance (Introduction) both push against a generic,
  interchangeable "Asian fantasy" treatment in favor of named, specific
  local myths and named real-world touchpoints (Singapore's Merlion,
  Vietnam-era communism, the 2015 refugee-adjacent "second Merlion").

# Changeling: The Dreaming 20th Anniversary Edition — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
This is the core rulebook for Changeling: The Dreaming 20th Anniversary
Edition (2017), the newest presentation of White Wolf/Onyx Path's 1995
game about Kithain — fae souls reincarnated into human bodies to survive
in a Banality-poisoned modern world. Structurally it is three "Books"
(Childling, Wilder, Grump) bundled in one volume: Book One covers setting
and character concept (cosmology, the 13 core Kiths, the 14 sidhe Noble
Houses, character creation); Book Two covers the magic and rules engine
(the 18 Arts, dice mechanics, combat, Banality/Bedlam); Book Three covers
the Dreaming itself as a place (trods, freeholds, chimera), Storytelling
advice, the setting's antagonists (Prodigals, Thallain, the Dauntain,
Autumn People), and two large appendices of non-European fae (the
Gallain: Nunnehi, Menehune, Hsien, Inanimae) and of mortals touched by
Glamour (Kinain, the Enchanted). Tonally the book insists Changeling is
not just whimsical: Banality, Bedlam, and the Undoing are treated as
real, sad, permanent losses, and several catalogs (Thallain, Dauntain,
Autumn People) exist specifically to make that loss mechanically and
narratively concrete.

## Front Matter (pp. 1-12)
Title/poem page, standard credits and dedication (to "Cyrile Monter, and
... all the other Dreamers who aren't with us anymore"), roughly 30
short first-person tribute/testimonial blurbs from Changeling fans and
Storytellers around the world (Ukraine, Chile, Portugal, Turkey, Russia,
Belgium, Germany, Greece, Peru, Mexico, and more) reflecting on what the
game has meant to them since 1995, and the full Table of Contents. No
mechanical or setting content; useful only as color on the game's global,
multi-generational fan community.

## Book One: Childling — Prelude: Both Sides of the Coin (pp. 13-25)
**Summary:** A short fiction piece (no crunch) that sets tone rather than
canon. Follows Baron Sevastyan and Baroness Zhanna of House Varich
("Kingdom of Concordia" nobility) in a glass-tower freehold, then two
young changelings — Ranveig (troll) and pooka teammates Tisket, Colbert,
Dolaidh, and Gibraltar — investigating a kidnapping via a night-club
scene. Demonstrates the tonal split the book keeps returning to: Seelie
court elegance/menace (the Varich siblings' icy, transactional affection)
against Unseelie/commoner street-level urgency (the young motley's
banter-under-pressure). Establishes House Varich and Eiluned as
politically active early, and sets up "Kingdom of Concordia" as the
default North American play space before Chapter One formally introduces
it.
**Key themes:** duality of noble/commoner registers, found-family motley
bonds, urban modern setting as default (not medieval).

## Introduction (pp. 26-31)
**Summary:** Explains the book's purpose as a "love letter" marking
Changeling's 20th anniversary — old material updated, not a metaplot
continuation. **Dream Diary: History of the Game** (p.26) gives real
publication history: 1st edition 1995 as a tonal counterpoint to
Vampire/Werewolf/Mage/Wraith; 2nd edition trimmed odd early rules; the
line moved to a smaller imprint in 1999 and was cancelled in 2001; a
2004 spinoff, *Dark Ages: Fae*, got one book before Classic World of
Darkness ended with *Time of Judgment*; this 2017 20th-anniversary
edition is the revival. Full chapter-by-chapter roadmap of the book by
"Book." A full **Lexicon** (p.27-28) defines every core term in one
place — Arcadia, Banality, Bedlam, Chimera, Chrysalis, the Dreaming
(Near/Far/Deep), the Escheat, Fae Mien, Gallain, Glamour, the Mists,
Mortal Seeming, Nightmare, Prodigal, Trods, Undoing, Voile, and the
three Seemings — making this the fastest single page to check for a
term's baseline definition. Closes with an **Inspirational Material**
list (Neil Gaiman, *Stardust*/*Neverwhere*, *Jonathan Strange & Mr
Norrell*, the October Daye series, *The Dark Crystal*, *Labyrinth*, *The
Neverending Story*, Miyazaki films, *Grimm*/*Once Upon a Time*/*Lost
Girl*).
**Key themes:** revival/legacy framing, terminological grounding.
**Key terms/entities:** full Lexicon (p.27-28), publication history
(p.26).

## Chapter One: A World of Darkness (pp. 32-83)
**Summary:** The setting-history and cosmology chapter, and the single
best chapter to read for broad "what is this game's world" questions.
**History** (p.33-42) runs as a connected timeline: the Mythic Age (the
malevolent Fomorians rule via the Elder Dark; humanity's suffering
"dreams forth" nature-spirits to resist them) → the Great Unleashing and
Tessarakonta/War of Trees → the Time of Legends (the Tuatha de Danaan,
led by Daana, defeat the Fomorians and depart, leaving Three Sacred
Oaths) → the Sundering (cold iron's discovery begins severing Dreaming
from Earth) → the Shattering (the last sidhe flee to Arcadia through
Silver's Gate; commoners left behind invent the Changeling Way to
survive in mortal bodies) → the Interregnum (the bleak centuries between)
→ the Resurgence (July 20, 1969 — the Moon Landing's burst of Glamour
lets the first Arcadian sidhe return via "glimmers," beginning with the
Battle of the Bay, the Beltaine Massacre, and the **Accordance War**
between returning nobility and the commoners who had self-governed for
600 years; ends when **David Ardry** of House Gwydion, wielding the
sword **Caliburn**, negotiates the **Treaty of Concord**, founding
**Concordia** and the **Parliament of Dreams**) → the **Evanescence**
(the September 11, 2001 nightmare-Glamour surge that lets the Thallain
and Dark-Kin flood back in force, ending the Silver Ban's protection) →
**Present Day**, where David Ardry has returned from a mysterious
disappearance (he vanished after meeting Faerilyth of House Eiluned;
his knight Sir Seif, an eshu, recovered Caliburn and eventually restored
him) but sits withdrawn and troubled while commoner-noble tension and
Shadow Court influence both rise. **The Nature of the Fae** (p.42-51)
covers Chrysalis (the awakening event, preceded by warning visions,
climaxing in the uncontrolled "Dream Dance" that spews chimera and
attracts both helpers and predators), Fosterage (the mentor-fledgling
Oath relationship, distinct from the Saining), the Saining (the yearlong
naming ritual that formally inducts a new changeling; nobles undergo the
harsher **Fior-Righ**), Banality and Glamour as opposed forces, the
Mists (originally a hiding-shield, now also what erodes changeling
memory), how changelings age (via *seeming*, not calendar time — tied to
mindset, reversible in rare cases; Arcadian sidhe alone did not adopt the
death-rebirth cycle, making mortality newly frightening to them), and
Mortal Seeming vs. Fae Mien. **Places of Glamour** (p.51-53) defines
Glades (natural, keyed to a sacred stone, one-harvester-only), Freeholds
(the balefire-powered social hub — see Ch.7 for full mechanics), and
Trods (the magical roads; unreliable and time-distorting after 600 years
of decay). **The Dreaming** (p.53-56) introduces the Near/Far/Deep
three-plane model (fully mechanized later in Ch.7) and the changeling
Courts. **Changeling Society** (p.62-65) covers Households vs. Motleys,
Liege & Vassal feudalism, and the full **Escheat** (six rights: Demesne,
to Dream, of Ignorance, of Rescue, of Safe Haven, of Life — each given
both its idealistic text and a cynical "Reality" gloss on how it's
actually honored). The chapter closes with a **60-page world gazetteer**
(p.64-83) — Concordia's eight North American Kingdoms and their duchies
down to named rulers and freeholds (e.g. Tara-Nar in the Catskills, seat
of the High King; Queen Mab of House Fiona in the Kingdom of Apples), then
a whistle-stop tour of fae kingdoms on every other continent (Albion,
Hibernia, Neustria/France, Iberia, the Galacian Confederation, the
Hellenic Empire, the Empire of the Firebird/Russia, the Caliphate of
Cedars, Africa's Lands of Ancient Tales, Australia's Land of the
Wandering Dream, even an Antarctic "Kingdom of Eternal Winter"). This
gazetteer is pure reference material — query book_chunks.db by
kingdom/duchy name for specific rulers and freeholds rather than reading
it as connected narrative.
**Key themes:** history as cycle of loss and partial recovery, duality
(Seelie/Unseelie, noble/commoner) as the organizing tension of the whole
setting, real-world historical trauma as a source of magical power (both
positive — the Moon Landing — and negative — 9/11).
**Key terms/entities:** the Sundering/Shattering/Interregnum/Resurgence/
Evanescence timeline (p.35-42), Caliburn and David Ardry (p.38-42), the
Treaty of Concord and Parliament of Dreams (p.39-40), the Escheat's six
rights (p.65), Near/Far/Deep Dreaming (p.53-56), Concordia gazetteer
(p.64-83).

## Chapter Two: The Kithain (pp. 84-139)
**Summary:** Character-concept chapter: the three Seemings, the 13 core
Kiths, and the 14 sidhe Noble Houses. This is the second-most load-bearing
chapter in the book for actual play and worth reading in full for anyone
building or evaluating a character.

**Seemings** (p.84-88): Childling (wonder-driven, +1 Glamour, most
Bedlam-prone), Wilder (adventure-driven, +1 Glamour or Willpower,
immune to Banality from chimerical violence during a quest), Grump
(duty/wisdom-driven, +1 Willpower, can spend time around high-Banality
sources without accruing it). Movement between the three is normally
one-directional but can reverse given a strong enough Glamour surge.

**The 13 Kiths** (p.88-114), each given appearance/lifestyle/affinity
Realm/Birthright/Frailty/stereotypes-of-others: **Boggans** (household
gossips/craftsmen, compulsively helpful — Frailty: *Call of the Needy*;
Affinity Actor); **Clurichaun** (leprechaun-descended music-and-brawling
collectors — Frailty: *Hoard*; Affinity Actor); **Eshu** (African-
originated wandering storytellers, "princes of the road" — Affinity
unlisted flavor-wise but function as social/travel specialists);
**Nockers** (foul-mouthed perfectionist inventors — Frailty: no
nocker-made device is ever without a flaw; Affinity Prop); **Piskies**
(shapeless-identity couriers/thieves who blend into any group — Frailty:
*Light-Fingers* compulsive pilfering; Affinity Actor); **Pooka**
(animal-bonded shapeshifting tricksters who must lie when asked a direct
question — Frailty: *Untruths*; Affinity Nature); **Redcaps** (monstrous,
can-eat-anything, universally feared and distrusted — Frailty: *Bad
Attitude*, social penalty; Affinity Nature); **Satyrs** (hedonist
warrior-poets bonded in extended "tragos" families — Frailty: *Passion's
Curse*, volatile temper; Affinity Fae); **Selkies** (seal skinchangers
who lose all fae power if parted from their coat, which if destroyed
kills their fae self permanently — Affinity Nature); **Arcadian Sidhe**
(the returning nobility, unearthly beauty, *Curse of Banality* doubles
all Banality gain — Affinity Time); **Autumn Sidhe** (sidhe who stayed
through the Interregnum via the Changeling Way, more human-relatable
beauty, *Adoration* Frailty risks obsessive mortal fixation — Affinity
Fae); **Sluagh** (secret-collecting recluses who can only ever whisper —
Affinity Prop); **Trolls** (honor-bound giant guardians, gain Strength
and health from swearing Oaths but sicken if they break one — Affinity
Fae). A **Kith Creation** system (p.114-119, worked example: the
domovoi) lets a troupe build new kiths from Acuity/Magic/Advantage
Birthright categories and Compulsion/Curse/Ineptitude Frailty categories.

**The 14 Noble Houses** (p.119-138), each with founding myth, Court,
nickname, Boon, Flaw, exile story, internal factions, and named notable
members: **Aesin** (Unseelie; Scandinavian nature-bonded, speak with
animals, cannot Rapture); **Ailil** ("Dragons," Unseelie political
masterminds, Shadow Court-adjacent, can't admit fault without penalty);
**Balor** ("Blooded," Unseelie brute-force house with visible Fomorian
deformities, immune to Glamour-loss from cold iron but still takes
aggravated damage from it, plans to conquer the Autumn World outright
rather than seek reinstatement to Arcadia); **Beaumayn** ("Dark Stars,"
Seelie prophets/monster-hunters, solitary, prophetic-vision Birthright,
Thallain/Shadow Court instinctively hunt them); **Danaan** (nominally
Unseelie, the only house that stayed almost entirely *in* the Dreaming
rather than returning to Earth — the only house with a non-sidhe High
Lord, recruits commoners freely, suffers creeping amnesia about the
mundane world); **Daireann** (Unseelie; herbalists/poisoners bound by
three laws of Honor/Hospitality/Vengeance, founded after Conchobhair was
abandoned at the closing gates of Arcadia); **Dougal** ("Smiths," Seelie
craft-and-DIY-technology patrons, members' bodies gradually replace
limbs with clockwork as they gain Title); **Eiluned** ("House of
Secrets," Seelie sorcerer/hacker house, compulsively investigates any
hidden thing, founded by prophetess Eiluned after her warnings to House
Ailil were ignored); **Fiona** ("Lions/True Hearts," Seelie
passion-and-courage house, fearless (cannot be magically frightened
except through threats to a lover), high commoner intermarriage/goodwill);
**Gwydion** ("Falcons," Seelie's most powerful/arrogant ruling house —
David Ardry's house — can sense lies via Kenning vs. Manipulation+
Subterfuge, must avenge any slight to House honor by the next sunrise or
suffer a Banality trigger); **Leanhaun** ("Muses," Unseelie patrons of
the arts who literally age backward on fresh Dreamer Glamour and forward
without it, feed via Ravaging/Rhapsody on the artists they inspire);
**Liam** ("Monks/Oathbreakers" — a slur — Seelie house exiled for
defending mortals' rights and dignity against sidhe who saw humans as
mere Glamour sources; cannot Ravage, begin with +1 Banality, can spend
Glamour to reduce a mortal's Banality or a changeling's Nightmare by
touch); **Scathach** ("Gray Walkers," nominally-Seelie warrior-elite
house that elevated commoners on merit and was pariah'd for it,
stealth/combat specialists, forbidden from using Sovereign if not
sidhe); **Varich** (Unseelie Russian pattern-readers/gamblers/
strategists, founded after a sluagh sorceress tricked founder Varich
into loving a snow-construct — members can never swear a love-oath).

**Key themes:** every house's founding myth doubles as its present-day
psychology (Daireann's abandonment-then-vengeance, Liam's exile-for-
compassion, Eiluned's ignored-warning paranoia); institutional
memory/grudge as the primary driver of noble politics; the recurring
split between houses that stayed through the Interregnum (Scathach,
Liam, some of Danaan) and those that fled to Arcadia and only recently
returned, which colors how each house relates to commoners.
**Key terms/entities:** all 13 kiths and their Frailties (p.88-114), all
14 houses and their Boons/Flaws (p.119-138), Kith Creation system
(p.114-119).

## Chapter Three: Character Creation & Traits (pp. 140-190)
**Summary:** Almost entirely mechanical (step-by-step character
creation, Attributes, Abilities, Backgrounds, Willpower, Glamour intro,
Merits and Flaws catalog) — not summarized trait-by-trait here; query
book_chunks.db directly for specific rules text. The one substantial
piece of *setting* content embedded in this chapter is **Personality
Archetypes: Legacies** (p.157-161): every changeling has a Seelie and an
Unseelie Legacy (one dominant per current Court), each with a Quest
(regain Willpower) and Ban (lose Willpower if violated). 24 Legacies
total are fully defined — Seelie: Bumpkin, Courtier, Crafter, Dandy,
Hermit, Orchid, Paladin, Panderer, Regent, Sage, Saint, Squire,
Troubadour, Wayfarer; Unseelie: Beast, Fatalist, Fool, Grotesque, Knave,
Outlaw, Pandora, Peacock, Rake, Riddler, Ringleader, Rogue, Savage,
Wretch. These function as this game's answer to V20's Paths of
Enlightenment or archetypal character "classes," and are reused
verbatim by Thallain (as a secondary Legacy), Kinain, and Enchanted
characters elsewhere in the book.
**Key themes:** N/A (mechanical chapter) apart from the Legacies system.
**Key terms/entities:** the 24 Legacies (p.157-161), Backgrounds catalog
including Chimera/Dreamers/Holdings/Remembrance/Title/Treasures
(p.168-172), Merits and Flaws (p.176-190).

## Chapter Four: Arts and Realms (pp. 194-241)
**Summary:** The magic-system chapter — mechanically dense (18 Arts x 5
power levels each, plus 6 Realms), comparable to V20's Discipline
catalog, and not summarized power-by-power here; query book_chunks.db
for specific cantrip text. Cantrips combine an Art (skill/power level)
with one or more Realms (target scope: Fae/Actor/Nature/Prop as primary,
Scene/Time as scope-modifiers only). "Bunks" (theatrical, often
self-endangering actions — the book gives dozens of examples from "loud
burp" at –1 difficulty to "skydiving without a parachute" at –5) lower
cantrip difficulty by symbolically feeding Banality-disruption into the
casting. "Unleashing" lets any changeling channel raw, uncontrolled
Glamour through an Art she knows without needing the matching Realm, at
the cost of Nightmare dice and a real risk of losing control.
Thematically, each Art carries a distinct flavor worth knowing even
without the power-by-power crunch: **Autumn** (decay/death/haunting,
historically a sluagh and Unseelie-boggan specialty, socially stigmatized
as "black magic"); **Chicanery** (illusion/memory-editing, historically
a commoner "rebellion against the Mists," long looked down on by elder
nobles); **Chronos** (time distortion — postcognition, aging/de-aging,
localized time-freeze, up to full Rip-Van-Winkle style dilation);
**Contract** (oath-magic — binding agreements with mechanical teeth,
from "Done Deal" sanctifying a promise to reflexive punishment of
oathbreakers); **Dragon's Ire** (combat-buff/destruction Art, martial
and honor-flavored, its capstone power channels "ancient warriors of
Arcadian legend"); **Legerdemain** (sleight-of-hand/illusion/
teleportation-of-objects, the "oldest and most widespread" commoner
Art); **Metamorphosis** (transformation — historically a pooka
specialty the sidhe consider "base magic"); **Naming** (True-Name
magic, ties directly into the Saining ritual and grants massive
difficulty reductions against a target whose True Name is known);
**Oneiromancy** (dream-walking/dream-manipulation, can pull mortals or
changelings bodily into dreams, possess a sleeping target's body via
Dream Puppet); **Primal** (raw elemental conjuration — fire, water,
stone, wind — from a wildflower to enough force to flip a car);
**Pyretics** (fire — heat, purification, a capstone "Phoenix Song" that
resurrects the Art's target on death); **Skycraft** (wind and weather,
speed-and-flight focused, favored by eshu and trolls alike);
**Soothsay** (divination/scrying/fate-reading, seer's Art, limited to
one attempt per specific fortune per story); **Sovereign** (command/
compulsion, historically the Art of royal authority, its Geas power lets
a sidhe issue a magically-binding command); **Spring** (growth/healing/
protective wards like the Faerie Ring); **Summer** (passion/desire/
emotional intensity, up to genuinely dangerous Aphrodisia-style
compulsion); **Wayfare** (movement/teleportation/portal-opening,
prized by scouts and couriers, ties directly into trod and gateway
mechanics in Ch.7); **Winter** (cold/fear/stasis, up to freezing a
target in magical stasis outside time). **Realms** (p.233-236): Actor
(power over people), Fae (power over fae/Dreaming things — its capstone
can affect Treasures, freeholds, and balefires directly), Nature (power
over the natural world, capstone affects weather/geology itself), Prop
(power over crafted objects, never cold iron), plus modifier-only Scene
(expands a cantrip to an area) and Time (delays/repeats a cantrip's
activation).
**Key themes:** N/A (mechanical chapter) — but note the recurring
class-coded pattern that certain Arts (Autumn, Chicanery, Legerdemain)
are historically "commoner" magic while others (Naming, Chronos,
Sovereign) were historically noble-restricted, echoing Ch.1/Ch.2's
noble/commoner tension.
**Key terms/entities:** Bunk difficulty-modifier table (p.195-196),
Unleashing rules (p.196-198), all 18 Arts (p.197-233), the 6 Realms
(p.233-236).

## Chapter Six: Systems and Drama (pp. 252-296)
**Summary:** Mixed mechanical/lore chapter, more setting-relevant than
Chapters Four or Five because several of its systems (Oaths, Banality,
Nightmare, Bedlam) are core to how the setting *feels* to play, not just
how it resolves in dice. **Dramatic Systems/Tempers** (p.252-264) covers
Feats and the **Five Paths to Epiphany** — the ways changelings gather
Glamour: *Ravaging* (forcibly stripping a mortal Dreamer's creativity,
with a full "Ravaging Thresholds" table of dark specific tactics —
Exhaust Creativity, Destroy Hope, Destroy Love, Create Anger, Break
Trust, Exploit Dependence, Destroy Illusions — this is presented as
genuinely predatory, not a morally-neutral option); *Revelry* (each kith's
own signature Glamour-gathering activity, described per-kith back in
Ch.2); *Rhapsody* ("Path of Destruction" — forbidden even by most
Unseelie — over-feeds a mortal artist Glamour until they burn out a
final masterpiece and are destroyed as a creator, sometimes becoming an
Autumn Person or an active enemy); plus Reverie and Bunks as more benign
paths mentioned but detailed elsewhere. **Oaths** (p.264-268): the full
system for how sworn promises get literal magical teeth — required
elements (gathering, exact wording, at least one Glamour point invested,
optional rule-of-three/nature-imagery/stated-consequence flourishes),
Restoration and Atonement for the forsworn, and eight fully worked
**Sample Oaths** with mechanical effects: Oath of Clasped Hands (blood-
bond friendship), Oath of Fealty (the formal knighting/Saining oath),
Oath of Escheat (liege accepting a vassal), Oath of the Accepted Burden
(promise to perform a task), Oath of Guardianship (troll-favored,
protect an object/place/person unto death), Oath of True Hearts
(marriage vows), Oath of the Long Road (quest-oath, the single most
potent oath in the book), and Oath of Crossed Blades (a formal
blood-feud declaration, mostly sworn by trolls and sidhe). **Banality**
(p.267-274) is given full mechanical texture: a 1-10 rating scale with
flavor text at every point (1-3 = "Dreamers," precious and rare; 9-10 =
functionally Autumn People) plus a note explicitly rejecting the idea
that low Banality correlates with mental illness; rules for Banal
locations/structures (a megachurch or "cubicle farm" can radiate
Banality strong enough to slowly convert nearby mortals); Banality
Triggers (a fixed list of acts, e.g. invoking the Autumn, killing a
changeling or Dreamer, wielding cold iron, breaking an oath) plus each
character's unique personal **Antithesis**; the Mists effect-duration
chart for both mortal witnesses and changelings; and **Quests**
(p.271-274, two worked types — Quest of Deed, retrieving a legendary
artifact from the Far Dreaming, and a second inspiration-focused Quest
type — as the *only* way to permanently shed a point of Banality once
gained, framed as rare and difficult). **Nightmare** (p.274-276) is the
dice-corruption mechanic tied to reckless Glamour use — Nightmare dice
replace normal dice in a cantrip pool and can misfire on a 10. **Combat**
(p.275-290) — four-phase turn structure (Initiative/Attack/Defense/
Resolution), close vs. ranged combat, full worked combat example.
**Health/Cold Iron/Death** (p.290-294): cold iron is specifically
wrought iron (not modern steel), illegal to forge or own almost
everywhere in Concordia, and inflicts aggravated damage to *both* a
changeling's chimerical and physical bodies simultaneously while
draining Glamour — the setting's single most dangerous ordinary weapon.
Chimerical death (temporary, causes Mists-amnesia) is distinguished from
Banal death (true, permanent Undoing). **Bedlam** (p.294-296): the
madness of over-exposure to the Dreaming, tracked via Imbalanced
Willpower and a menu of "Bedlam Thresholds" (Whispers and Visions,
Memories from a Dream, Weird as the Wyrd, Untrustworthy, Thickened Air,
Unstable Dreams, Remove the Leash, To Thine Own Self, Drowning in
Glamour) that each impose a specific narrative-plus-mechanical penalty;
full Bedlam has no known cure.
**Key themes:** Banality and Bedlam as twin, opposite failure states
that define the changeling condition (too much mundane world vs. too
much Dreaming, both fatal to the fae self); Oaths as the load-bearing
social technology of Kithain society, mechanically enforced rather than
just roleplayed; Ravaging/Rhapsody as an explicit moral hazard built
into the Glamour-economy, not a neutral resource mechanic.
**Key terms/entities:** Five Paths to Epiphany (p.259-264), full Oaths
system and 8 sample oaths (p.264-268), Banality 1-10 scale and Triggers
(p.267-270), the Mists chart (p.269), Quests as Banality-removal
(p.271-274), cold iron mechanics (p.291-292), Bedlam Thresholds
(p.294-296).

## Chapter Seven: The Dreaming (pp. 300-327)
**Summary:** The Dreaming-as-a-place chapter — cosmology mechanized into
play rules, and one of the richest chapters for both broad questions and
specific lookups. **Dream Mechanics** (p.300-306): the Near/Far/Deep
three-plane structure gets full rules (each plane changes cantrip
difficulty, Unleashing dice, Glamour regeneration rate, and even
Birthright/Frailty severity — a full "how strong is a Wyrd effect for
your kith at this depth" table is given for each plane). The **Vale of
Mists** connects Near and Far Dreaming; the **Mistweir** is the final
barrier before the Deep Dreaming, guarded by 13 magical gates (14 before
one collapsed during the Evanescence); the ancient hidden city of **Fin
Bheara** sits at the Mistweir's edge, ruled jointly by a sidhe queen and
its boggan founder in alternating half-years. Deep Dreaming visitors
undergo a "second Chrysalis," their fae miens intensifying toward their
pre-Shattering power level. **The Mists of Forgetfulness** (p.303-306)
gives full entry/exit charts cross-referencing Glamour and Banality
against memory effects, both for the traveling changeling and for
anyone she brings along. **Trods** (p.307-311): three categories — the
**Silver Path** (protected by the Tuatha's ancient ban against Dark-kin
and Thallain, who take ongoing Glamour/health damage to use it), the
**Paths of Balor** (older, one-way, dark-fae roads subdivided into
Black/Green/Gold by destination — Gold paths, the rarest, supposedly
lead to Fomorians or the Tuatha themselves and nobody has ever
returned), and **Twilight Trods** (usable by anyone, largely controlled
by House Balor). Three fully worked example trods are given (Ripper's
Road, a Jack-the-Ripper-themed London Twilight trod controlled by House
Balor; McSwiggan's Digital Highway, a portable trod anchored to a
laptop; the lost Shimmering Trails). **Freeholds** (p.311-316): built
around a balefire-powered wellspring, with a full 5-step creation
process (Wellspring → Rath → Balefire → Glamour investment → shaping)
and named freehold-type archetypes (Hearth, Stronghold, Thorpe, Manor,
Market, Homestead, Workshop, plus Lost One and Nunnehi freeholds).
**Chimera** (p.315-322) get a full taxonomy — Incidental vs. Dreamed,
Inanimate vs. Animate, Non-sentient vs. Sentient — plus rules for
crafting chimerical items (a full difficulty/success-threshold table
mirroring real-world crafting, from "simple" to "masterwork") and eight
listed ways a chimera can come into being (Chrysalis-spawned,
emotion-spawned, Bedlam-spawned, Prodigal-spawned, humanity's collective
unconscious, Dreamer-spawned, freehold-spawned, chimera-spawned).
**Treasures** (p.322-326) distinguishes crafted (an Art's power built
into an object during creation) from imbued (built by ritual study of
an existing meaningful object) Treasures, with two fully statted named
Treasures (Vulcan's Hammer, Lightning Gun).
**Key themes:** the Dreaming as a literal, navigable, increasingly
dangerous-with-depth geography rather than an abstract "elsewhere";
memory/forgetting as a graduated, chart-driven mechanic rather than a
binary; the persistent motif of decayed infrastructure (trods, gates,
the Mistweir) that once worked better before the Sundering/Shattering.
**Key terms/entities:** Near/Far/Deep Dreaming mechanics (p.300-306),
the Mistweir and Fin Bheara (p.307-308), the Three Roads/trod types
(p.309-311), Freehold creation and types (p.311-316), Chimera taxonomy
and creation (p.315-320), Treasures (p.322-326).

## Chapter Nine: Nightmares & Stranger Things (pp. 346-392)
**Summary:** The antagonists/threats chapter and the single richest
chapter in the book for "who or what opposes changelings" questions —
covers Prodigals (other World of Darkness supernaturals), original
Nightmare Chimera, the Dauntain (corrupted changelings), the Harbingers
of Autumn (Banality's mortal agents), and the Thallain (evil fae, a
massive 19-kith catalog).

**Prodigals** (p.346-357): other World of Darkness supernaturals are
mechanically represented as having "equivalent Arts" rather than their
native power systems. **Vampires** — three competing changeling myths
of vampire origin are given without the book endorsing one (a jealous
redcap; a cursed Thallain named Lilith; a starving sidhe offshoot);
Kithain lore singles out Tremere and Kiasyd as actively dangerous to
changelings (the Kiasyd, statted in detail, are themselves the result of
a vampire's alchemical diet of changeling blood). Statted examples:
Toreador Neonate, Kiasyd Ancilla, Ravnos Elder. **Werewolves** — framed
as the Dreaming's "most tragic loss," exiled by the Tuatha for refusing
single-form obedience; Get of Fenris are sworn sidhe-killers over an
ancient broken geas, while Fianna are the closest werewolf allies
(shared Kinain bloodlines are hinted at). Statted: Get of Fenris
Adolescent, Black Spiral Dancer Veteran, Fianna Elder. **Magi** — six
named philosophical factions given (Marauders, Mystics, True Believers,
Modern Cynics, Technocrats, and implicitly Traditions/Ecstatics/Akashics),
ranging from Glamour-rich "half-enchanted" Mystics to pure-Banality
Technocrats who "want [changelings] wiped from the Earth." Statted:
Sahajiya Cultist (Cult of Ecstasy), Akashic Master, Technocratic
Cyber-Freak. **Wraiths** — live in the Shadowlands, bound by Fetters
and Passions, split between a rational self and a malevolent inner
"Shadow" that can fully consume a wraith into a **Spectre**. Statted:
Lemure Harbinger, Long Dead Sandman, Murderous Spectre. **Cousins Long
Removed** (p.354-357) — a short catch-all for rarer Prodigals: Mummies
(the Undying, former enchanted mortals bound by oath to Arcadian
patrons, functionally indestructible, balance-obsessed) and Demons (per
House Balor's account, ancient fae exiled to a Deep Dreaming labyrinth
during the last Winter, escaping in increasing numbers as "Winter
approaches again" — possess mortal hosts, cannot possess changelings or
Prodigals).

**Nightmare Chimera** (p.357-360): four categories — **Feral Chimera**
(wild-animal counterparts, e.g. Fairy Cats, Cù Sìth/black dogs,
Hellsteeds); **Nervosa** (urban, unpredictable, born of crowded-city
paranoia, e.g. Plague); **Nocnitsa** (the most dangerous/diverse
category — includes named statted examples like the self-aware nocker
creation Iteration 42/"Phil," Skittering Terrors, Cryptids); **Urban
Legends** (recent pop-culture-spawned chimera — Mannequins, five
archetypal **Outsiders**: Implacable Monsters, Smiling Men, Shrieks,
Unbound Shadows, Harbingers).

**The Dauntain** (p.359-364): changelings whose Chrysalis went wrong,
"the Ruined of Arcadia," each marked with a **Brand** tied to their
specific doom — Brand of the Cadaver (plague-carrier), Brand of the
Forsaken (abandoned in the Dreaming, can't feed from nightmare-free
Dreamers), Brand of the Iconoclast (must destroy something of the
Dreaming daily or suffer penalties), Brand of the Lich (bound to a
phylactery, can be enslaved if it's stolen), Brand of the Quisling
(oathbreakers rejected by the Dreaming itself, chimera attack on
sight), Brand of the Unholy (a personal curse-trigger). Dauntain use a
degraded, incomplete Art called **Ruin** (full cantrip catalog given:
Cold Iron Infusion, Poison the Well, Salt the Earth, Broken Oath,
Burning in Effigy) with reduced power over Nature/Prop Realms. Two fully
statted example Dauntain given: Erik Mikelson "The Broken Knight" (a
Lost Arcadian sidhe) and "Shadow" (a Typhoid pooka).

**The Harbingers of Autumn** (p.363-367): **Autumn People** are
ordinary-seeming, high-Banality mortals with unconscious offensive
"Stigmas" — Aversion Therapy (implants phobias about the Dreaming),
Erasure (drains Glamour by mere proximity), Lingering Doubt (corrodes
self-worth via criticism), Numb (drains Willpower through dull
conversation), Rationalization (actively disbelieves cantrips and
chimera into failing), Scorn (destroys a target's confidence in a
creative project). A fully statted example (the Theater Critic) is
given. **Bastions of Autumn** (p.366-367) are two named real-world-style
organizations that institutionalize anti-Glamour "treatment": the
Bellerophon Foundation (a fictional pseudo-medical institute that
invented "pervasive paracosm-fixation disorder" to pathologize seeing
the Dreaming) and the Renewal in Faith Fellowships ("pray the fae away"
camps run by a televangelist).

**Stranger Things** (p.367-369): **The Undone** are fully Undone
changelings who retain unsettling fragmentary memories, effectively
amnesiac rather than mad — enchanting one is explicitly called out as a
catastrophic mistake (it drains the enchanter's Glamour with no benefit
to the Undone). **Dream Eaters/Cold Iron Chimera** are chimera that have
somehow gained a Banality rating of their own, created (per folklore) in
the twisted Chrysalises of tormented Dauntain, and self-destruct via
their own accumulating Banality; two named legendary Banal Treasures
(the Friar's Cross, the Gray Sword) are described as bounty-worthy
threats the High King wants destroyed.

**Thallain** (p.369-392): the book's second-largest catalog. **History**
(p.369-370): the Thallain are the Fomorians' original creation, servants
of the primordial Elder Dark predating the Tuatha de Danaan; defeated in
the Tessarakonta/War of Trees and bound by the Silver Ban, they slip
back to Earth in small numbers during historic mass-atrocity events
("Tarraroms" — the Black Plague, the slave trade, the World Wars,
Vietnam) with the September 11, 2001 Evanescence finally burning out the
last of the Ban. The **Black Court** (a Thallain-infiltrated inner
circle secretly running the nominally-Unseelie **Shadow Court**, per a
sidebar back in Ch.1) is their primary present-day power base.
**Seemings** (p.370): perturbed/prowler/miser instead of childling/
wilder/grump. **Nightmare Legacies** (p.371-372, primary Legacy for
every Thallain, paired with a secondary Kithain Legacy) — Alpha, Black
Widow, Brute, Charlatan, Devourer, Judas, Gatekeeper, Mimic, Omega,
Parasite, Serpent, Strategist, Terror, Tormentor. **19 Thallain Kiths**
(p.371-392), each a dark mirror of a Kithain (or Gallain) counterpart,
given in compact form here (full appearance/Endowment/Vulnerability
detail is in the chunk DB): Aithu (eshu-cousin hypnotic storyteller-
predators), Beasties (pooka-cousin, forced monthly to "the Hunt"),
Bodachs (clurichaun-cousin child-terrorizing house-fae, physically
fragile), Boggarts (boggan-cousin mercenary kleptomaniac packs), Bogies
(sluagh-cousin organ/fluid-craving urban predators), Dark-kin (the
broader Denizen category — see Appendix I cross-reference below, p.383),
Ghasts (redcap-cousin, methodical organ-black-market surgeons), Goblins
(nocker-cousin reckless mechanical saboteurs, hold seasonal weapon
"Fashion Weeks"), Huaka'i Po (Hawaiian night-marcher warriors devoted to
the Fomorians), Kelpies (selkie-cousin drowning-predator water horses),
Lurks (huge, adaptable wilderness cryptid-cousins — Bigfoot/yeti/yowie
legends), Mandragoras (ghille-dhu-cousin carnivorous tree-possessing
ambush predators), Merrow (self-replicating drowned-sailor-spawned
ocean horrors), Nasties (satyr-cousin degenerate hedonists, satyrs
themselves hate them), Night Hags (sleep-paralysis-demon predators,
photophobic), Ogres (troll-cousin honorless brutes, some — ettins —
cunning), Sevartal (sidhe-cousin charismatic subjugators who
forcibly/prematurely enchant unawakened changelings into their service),
Skinwalkers (Native-American-lore predators who gain animal tokens
through atrocity), Spriggans (child-kidnapping treasure-hoarders, allied
with sevartal), Weeping Wights (coerce victims into fulfilling fatal
"legend" accident-scenarios). **Belief and Governance/Denizens and the
Autumn World** (p.383-386) introduces **Dark-kin/Denizens** as a
separate category from Thallain proper — direct Fomorian/Tuatha
descendants organized not by Court but by three-way **Ariá** (Dioniae/
instinct, Araminae/balance, Apolliae/higher-purpose — paired to
Legacies) — and **Denizen Creation** (p.385-392) statting seven
**Adhene** ("kiths"): Acheri (disease/temptation spirits), Aonides
("muses" who Ravage via seduction), Fir-bholg (antlered, primitive,
demand sacrifice for Glamour), Fuath (permanent-animal-feature,
frenzy-prone), Keremet (emotionally-deadened reincarnated-mortal
wraith-cousins bound by the unbreakable Pact of Dagda), Moiræ (Oracle/
Fury/Whimsy Fate-spirits protected on the Silver Path), Naraka
(Kali-descended, dance-compelled, multi-armed warrior spirits).
**Key themes:** every antagonist category in this chapter is a mirror
or inversion of something already established for the Kithain (Thallain
mirror Kiths, Dauntain are what changelings themselves become when
broken, Autumn People are ordinary mortals rather than monsters);
institutional/bureaucratic evil (the Technocracy, Bellerophon
Foundation, Renewal in Faith camps) treated as at least as dangerous as
supernatural evil; the persistent idea that loss in this setting is
usually not violent death but erasure/forgetting.
**Key terms/entities:** Prodigal stat blocks and factions (p.346-357),
Nightmare Chimera taxonomy (p.357-360), the five Brands of the Dauntain
and the Art of Ruin (p.359-364), Autumn People Stigmas (p.363-367), the
Black Court (p.62-63 cross-ref and p.369-370), 19 Thallain kiths
(p.371-383), the Ariá and 7 Denizen Adhene (p.383-392).

## Appendix I: Gallain (pp. 393-454)
**Summary:** Non-European fae — the book's largest single catalog and,
alongside Chapter Two and Chapter Nine, essential reading for anyone
running a game touching non-Concordia cultures. Covers four major
Gallain families in turn: Nunnehi (the Americas), Menehune (Hawai'i),
Hsien (East Asia), and Inanimae (elemental fae found worldwide).

**Nunnehi** (p.393-411): Native American fae ("people who live
anywhere"), who harvest **Medicine** instead of Glamour and are cut off
from the Dreaming's "Higher Hunting Grounds." **History** (p.393-397)
runs parallel to and intertwined with the history of the Native American
tribes themselves — early amicable contact with Norse-descended
European fae, worsening relations and outright warfare as later European
settlement, disease, and forced displacement devastated the mortal
tribes the Nunnehi are bonded to; a long roster of specific historical
tribes and confederacies is given by region (Iroquois Confederacy,
Wabanaki Confederation, Southeast/Southwest/Far West/Far North &
Pacific Northwest peoples) as reference material for grounding a
specific Nunnehi's origin. Politically, the Resurgence's return of the
sidhe re-opened old wounds (some Nunnehi allied with commoners against
the returning nobility, some stayed neutral, a few allied with the
sidhe). **Camps** (p.397-398) replace Seelie/Unseelie with Winter
(Rock)/Summer (Dogwood)/Midseason (Laurel — itself split into
Trickster and Watcher sub-roles) — visibly worn rather than concealed,
unlike Kithain court affiliation. **11 Kiths** given (p.397-406):
Canotili (stealthy forest hunters' helpers, Terror-pheromone Frailty),
Inuas (shamanic animal-form spirit-guides, vulnerable to pollution),
Kachinas (ritual-bound Southwest desert spirits, Cloud Form),
May-may-gwya-shi (Abnaki fish-thief tricksters, obsessive fish appetite),
Nanehi (vain, beautiful, tradition-preserving helpers — the namesake
of "Nunnehi" itself), Nümüzo'ho (giant environmental-justice avengers,
permanently disfigured), Pu'gwis (tragically hideous, once-beautiful,
starved for companionship, hypnotic Song of Beckoning), Rock Giants
(matrilineal, stone-armored, honor-bound to violence once provoked),
Surems (Yaqui-descended pacifists who claim to have warned tribes of
Europeans' coming), Tunghat (goblin-like animal-caretaker spirits,
increasingly embittered at humans' disrespect), Water Babies
(child-protecting/avenging, must immerse in water every 48 hours or
die). **Legacies** (p.406-409, Summer/Winter/Midseason, distinct from
Kithain Legacies) and **Totems** (p.409-411, e.g. Birch, Cottonwood,
Dogwood, Fir, Fireweed, Granite, Maize, Saguaro Cactus, Sandstone,
Tobacco, Whitewater — a Background granting Traits and imposing a
nature-respecting Taboo).

**Menehune** (p.411-421): the fae of Hawai'i, closer kin to the Nunnehi
than to the Kithain, gather Mana (Glamour) from nature via Totem
("Aumakua") spirits. **History** (p.411-415) is unusually specific and
grim: untouched by European Banality until Captain Cook's 1778 arrival
(his death and its bloody aftermath are recounted), followed by
Kamehameha's unification, missionary/whaler contact, Queen Ka'ahumanu's
deliberate breaking of the kapu taboo system (which nearly destroyed
Menehune society when no divine punishment followed), and eventual
rescue by a nocker whaler, **Jack Doggins**, who taught the survivors
the Changeling Way — he is revered with a dedicated heiau (temple) and
rumored to have been sacrificed to seal the ritual. **Camp** (Feast vs.
War, no transitional camp) and **Calling** (a fixed, birth-ordained
caste replacing kith: **Ali'i** — sidhe-equivalent nobility who store
communal Mana as living balefires, taboo against anyone stepping on
their shadow; **Kahuna** — priest-diviners who regulate the kapu system
and may break taboo once per lunar month without penalty; **Hana** —
boggan-equivalent commoner craftspeople/laborers, the calling's "heart";
**Kokua** — warrior-scouts organized into totem-named societies (shark
being most prestigious), prone to battle-cruelty). Village of
**Moe'uhane** described as the primary present-day Menehune settlement.

**Hsien** (p.421-437): East Asian "little gods," cosmologically distinct
from Western fae — created by the August Personage of Jade as Heaven's
earthly representatives, exiled after the Yama Kings corrupted the
celestial bureaucracy (the "Ages" framework: Perfection → Beauty →
Legends → Beautiful Sadness → present-day Tribulation → an unknown,
hoped-for Sixth Age). Reincarnate via **kun shou**, occupying a dying
mortal's body (hsien-tsu if a child, unaware until adulthood; hsien-jin
if an adult, aware immediately) — each rebirth costs memory. Powered by
**Chi** (their own substance, sacrificing it causes aggravated damage)
and **Yugen** (external power drawn from mortal prayer/celebration,
concentrated in **dragon nests**). Organized into political **Courts**
rather than a Seelie/Unseelie split (p.422-423): Li Shen (Confucian
Traditionalists), Xian Mo (mystical Quiet Ones), Yü (feudal
Classicists), Xian Mun (hedonist "Worldly," the Shinma-equivalent of
Unseelie disdain), Wu Hsien (rural Peasants), Tu Shen (joy-seeking
"Ambassadors of Heaven"), Shu Shen ("Profane Gods," closest Shinma
analog to Unseelie extremism, drawn to Kuei-jin), plus the secret Yellow
Lotus society and the corrupted Mu Courts (Daityas/Kura Sau, who serve
or serve-then-abandon the Yama Kings). Aspected to **Yin or Yang**
rather than Seelie/Unseelie, expressed via matched Legacy pairs. **12
Kwannon-jin** ("kiths," p.426-431+) given with Fortune (elemental
alignment), Luck, and Curse: Fu Hsi (snake-form marriage counselors),
Hanumen (monkey-king tricksters/thieves), Heng Po (unpredictable
"river of life" beings), Chu-ih-yu (metal-skinned, incapable of lying,
gravitate to law enforcement), Chu Jung (fire-elemental, law-bending),
Hou-Chi (wood-elemental forest guardians), Komuko (earth-elemental,
serene, socially difficult), Nyan (cat-spirits, clever and
self-interested), Suijen (water-elemental, all-perceiving, socially
reserved), Tanuki (raccoon-dog tricksters, unusually loyal). The magic
system, **Wu Tan** (p.431-437), is five elemental alchemies (Di/Hou/
Lin/Tieh/Shui Tan) plus **Nei Tan** internal alchemy (five ranks: T'ai
Su, T'ai I, T'ai Chi, Yu T'un, Wu Chi — the highest letting a Hsien
open portals into any spirit realm, a partial step toward regaining
Heaven's favor).

**Inanimae** (p.437-454): elemental fae souls who never left the
Autumn world during the Sundering/Shattering, instead entering
protective **Somnolence** in natural **Anchors** until stirred. Two
Courts by Anchor type: **Gladelings** (natural, untouched Anchors) vs.
**Krofted** (human-crafted-object Anchors, distrusted by Gladelings as
corrupted, held in truce since the **Making War** — a past conflict
between Gladeling/Krofted-allied phyla and the Solimonds/Empire of
Flames). Inanimae wear **Husks** (aging mortal-seeming façades) to
interact with the Autumn world and wield **Slivers** (elemental magic,
only Unleashed, never cast as ordered cantrips) tied to their **Slow
Empire** homeland. **6 core Phyla** given in full (p.440-445): Glomes
(Empire of Stones, patient stone-generals, Petros Sliver), Kuberas
(Empire of Seeds, sensual "sidhe of inanimae society," Verdage Sliver),
Ondines (Empire of Tears, shy, permanently-disabled land-exiled
water-spirits, Aquis Sliver), Parosemes (Empire of Skies, restless
wind-spirits, Stratus Sliver), Solimonds (Empire of Flames, passionate,
oath-bound fire-spirits historically blamed for the Making War, Pyros
Sliver), Mannikins (Empire of Dolls/Flickers, uniquely able to also
wield true Kithain Arts, infiltrate human society as mannequins/toys).
**8 "Other Kiths"** given more briefly (p.446-454): Ghille Dhu (seasonal
nature-spirits who rapidly age through seemings and die at Banality's
touch), Korred (secretive lore-keeper truth-tellers, cannot lie), Merfolk
(deep-ocean Tuatha-descended purists, "Coldness"/Banality nearly lethal
to them), Morganed (siren-like ocean-advocate seducers), Oba (secret
royal rulers of the eshu, bound to African/Indian/Middle Eastern
homeland soil), River Hags (redcap-cousin drowning predators tied to a
single river), Wichtel (miner/laborer earth-spirits, vengeful if
wronged), Wolpertinger (jackalope/skvader-legend hybrid chimera
recently promoted to full kith status by Resurgence-era Glamour).
**Key themes:** every Gallain family gets its own complete cosmology
rather than being a reskinned version of Kithain lore — distinct origin
myths, distinct currencies (Medicine/Mana/Yugen/Anchors instead of
uniform Glamour), distinct social structures (Camps, Callings, Courts,
Ariá) — while all still ultimately answer to the same Banality/Bedlam
threat; real-world colonial history (the Nunnehi's tribal
displacement, Menehune's Captain Cook encounter) treated with more
specificity and weight than the book's otherwise-fictional history.
**Key terms/entities:** Nunnehi Camps/11 kiths/Totems (p.397-411),
Menehune history and Ali'i/Kahuna/Hana/Kokua Callings (p.411-419), Hsien
Courts and 12 Kwannon-jin and Wu Tan (p.421-437), Inanimae Anchors/
Gladeling-Krofted split/6 core Phyla/Slivers (p.437-447), 8 further
Inanimae kiths (p.447-454).

## Appendix II: The Enchanted (pp. 455-464)
**Summary:** Rules and lore for changeling-adjacent mortals — the
**Kinain** (fae-blooded children of changelings) and the **Enchanted**
(mortals magically granted temporary or lasting access to the
Dreaming). **Kinain Lore** (p.455-460): a Kinain's fae "heritage" (their
changeling parent's kith) is unpredictable and can skip generations;
Kinain cannot be Undone or lose Glamour-memory to the Mists, making
them uniquely stable bridges between the mortal and fae worlds but also
uniquely vulnerable to watching a changeling parent be Undone or fall to
Bedlam while remaining unable to follow; historically served as
servants/go-betweens to nobility, a role many now resist post-
Resurgence. **Kinain Mechanics** (p.459-461): a capped 2-point Glamour
pool that self-regenerates (no harvesting needed); the **Faerie Blood**
Background caps how many Arts (not how high) a Kinain can learn;
Banality has no personal effect on a Kinain (tracked only for how
changelings perceive them). **Enchanted Lore/Mechanics** (p.461-464):
two methods of opening a mortal's eyes — **Temporary Enchantment**
(spend Glamour exceeding the target's Banality, fades at a fixed daily
rate, no Bestowments) versus the **Ritual of Parted Mists** (a
whole-motley 20-Glamour investment at a balefire, granting permanent
access plus kith-flavored **Bestowments** — Cry For Aid, Just In Time,
Tinker, Heart of Fear, Animal Speech, Incredible Might, Courtly Grace,
This Is True, Soothing Aura — until the mortal's Glamour hits zero, at
which point they are permanently un-enchantable and lose the memories
entirely). The appendix explicitly flags **Autumn Kinain** (fae-blooded
people who turned out ordinary-to-hostile toward the Dreaming, up to and
including allying with Bellerophon) and **Winter Kinain** (the single
most dangerous Kinain outcome — self-replenishing living founts of
Banality who never burn out) as recurring antagonist options, and
notes Dauntain-Blooded children as a specific tragic edge case.
**Key themes:** stability (Kinain, the Enchanted) purchased at the cost
of never fully belonging to either world; the throughline, present since
Ch.1's Escheat, that changelings' use of mortals carries real ethical
weight the book keeps insisting on rather than treating as free resource
extraction.
**Key terms/entities:** Faerie Blood Background (p.461), Temporary
Enchantment vs. Ritual of Parted Mists (p.461-462), Bestowments catalog
(p.462-464), Autumn/Winter Kinain (p.460).

## Cross-cutting themes (whole book)
- **Duality as the organizing principle of everything**: Seelie/
  Unseelie (Ch.1, Ch.3's Legacies), noble/commoner (Ch.1's Accordance
  War, Ch.2's Houses), Glamour/Banality (Ch.1, Ch.6), Bedlam/Undoing as
  opposite failure states (Ch.6) — nearly every system in the book is
  built as a spectrum between two poles rather than a single axis.
- **Loss is usually forgetting, not dying**: the Mists, Undoing, the
  Undone, Autumn People, and the entire structure of Chimerical vs.
  Banal death (Ch.6) all frame the setting's central tragedy as
  *erasure* — memories fading, a fae self quietly going out — far more
  than as violent combat death, which the game treats as almost the
  kinder outcome (chimerical death is explicitly "an acceptable final
  resolution" to disputes, p.318).
- **Institutional memory as present-day psychology**: nearly every Noble
  House's founding myth (Ch.2), the Thallain's Tarraroms tied to real
  historical atrocities (Ch.9), and the Nunnehi/Menehune's colonial
  histories (App.I) are written so that a group's origin trauma directly
  explains its current politics and taboos, not as inert backstory.
- **Real-world history as a Glamour/Banality engine**: the Moon Landing
  (Resurgence), 9/11 (Evanescence), the Black Plague and slave trade
  (Tarraroms), and Captain Cook's arrival in Hawai'i (Menehune history)
  are all treated as literal in-fiction magical events, not just flavor
  — this is a distinctive structural choice that ties the setting's
  timeline tightly to the real 20th/21st century rather than an
  invented calendar.
- **Every antagonist category mirrors something already established for
  the Kithain**: Thallain kiths mirror Kithain/Gallain kiths one-for-one
  (Ch.9), the Dauntain are what a changeling herself becomes when broken
  (Ch.9), Autumn People are ordinary humans rather than monsters
  (Ch.9) — the book consistently locates its horror in corruption/
  failure of the familiar rather than in wholly external monsters.
- **Class tension embedded in the magic system itself**: certain Arts
  are flagged as historically "commoner" (Chicanery, Legerdemain,
  Autumn) versus historically noble-restricted (Naming, Chronos,
  Sovereign) (Ch.4), directly extending the noble/commoner conflict from
  Ch.1/Ch.2 into the mechanics of magic itself.
